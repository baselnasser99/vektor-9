# Style guide | Vektor-9

The source of truth for every visual decision. When code and this guide disagree, fix one of them on purpose and note it here.

## 1. Mood

**Neon cyberpunk night, richly detailed** (changed 2026-11-03; was daylight anime cyberpunk). Dark gunmetal and charcoal structure (steel cages, trusses, ribbed metal, dark concrete, pipes, cables) lit warm first: sodium street lamps, amber LED edges, warm lit windows, red and pink neon; cyan and violet only as accents. A crimson sunset over an endless megacity (sky changed 2026-11-06; was deep blue night): burgundy to near-black sky, burnt orange and scarlet at the horizon, sweeping dark storm clouds with blood-red edges; the glow sits behind the far skyline only, and the city in front stays predominantly cold blue and teal. That separation, not a red filter over everything, is what makes the city feel enormous. Enormous, oppressive and moody, graded like a film, never a cartoon red. Streets are wet, with warm lights smearing into soft streaks, but surfaces are not chrome. Most windows are dark at night; lit rooms are a mix of dim, moderate and a few bright (see Windows). Edges are rounded, never knife-sharp. No building is a plain colored block. The daylight palette below stays for a possible day theme.

The reference for level of detail is the dense, hand-built look of modern city-builder games. Only the level of detail and technique are borrowed: no names, logos, characters, specific building designs, or screenshots from any existing show, game, or film appear in the project or the repo. Sign text is always invented.

## 3. Lighting and rendering

- **Materials:** physically based (`MeshStandardMaterial`) with CC0 texture sets from Poly Haven (color, normal, AO/roughness/metalness), tinted with palette colors. Painted steel uses flat palette color plus the worn-metal relief. Textures are mapped in metres (one repeat per 4 m). See ADR-0004.
- **Night lighting:** a cool sky fill with a cold teal fill on the shadow side, strong enough that facade detail reads without looking like day, a soft blue moon for shape and soft shadows, sodium street lamps with real warm lights, and a few point lights at the strongest neon so color spills onto walls and the street. Bloom gives lamps and neon soft halos.
- **Reflections:** low. The environment map is captured once from the finished scene at low intensity; steel and glass are mostly non-metallic and fairly rough (roughness 0.4 or more); window glass is never metallic (a metal pane loses its diffuse and goes black from most angles), and the street is wet but not a mirror (roughness about 0.55).
- **Surfaces:** no visibly tiling patterns. Concrete is a cast-concrete texture drawn in code (soft blotches, rain streaks, faint formwork seams). Round machinery is turned (lathe) geometry with tube supports, not boxes attached to cylinders.
- **Edges:** boxes use rounded geometry (radius up to 0.3 m); the post-processing target is multisampled so edges stay smooth.
- **Sun:** warm white directional light from high up, casting soft shadows. A sun-glare sprite and a few drifting sparkles sit in the sun's direction.
- **Shadows:** cool, slightly blue (hemisphere light with warm sky / dusty ground, plus a bluish ambient).
- **Atmosphere:** squared exponential fog with a small linear part, taking the sky's colour in each direction: foreground buildings stay sharp, mid-distance ones lose some contrast, distant ones fade into the sky. The haze is cold blue looking across the city and the street-level mist cold teal; it warms to blood-red smog only in the last few degrees below the horizon (where that starts wanders a little, so it is never one even line), so the glow sits behind the far skyline and never tints the city in front. Below the horizon the fog also turns red-violet `#3a1c2c` with distance (from about 900 m, full by 2.6 km), so near buildings stay crisp and cool, the far side of the city is a little hazy, and the far sprawl melts into red-violet; this lets go just above the smog so the horizon itself is the sky's. The fog's density is taken a quarter of the way up the sight line from its low end, so from high up the eye looks through thin air most of the way and aerial views see the far city, while along the streets nothing changes. Never so thick that materials look washed out. This is a large part of the look.
- **Bloom:** restrained; the threshold sits above the sky's brightness, so only emissive parts (signs, LEDs, reactor cores) glow.
- **Contact shading:** from texture AO and shadows for now; a screen-space AO pass washed the frame out and is deferred.
- **Outlines:** thin, low-opacity navy lines on architecture. Bold dark lines are reserved for small focal objects, if used at all.

## 4. Detail (the current sketch is far too plain)

Plain boxes in flat colors are the main gap. Priorities, roughly in order:

1. **Facade textures** generated in code: floor banding, panel grids, window rows, mullions, balconies, ad screens, plus grime, streaks, and weathering. Several variants per facade family.
2. **Silhouettes:** setbacks, podium plus tower, stepped tops, cylinders wrapped in banded ad screens, bridges between towers, stacked billboard frames, overhangs.
3. **Rooftops:** water tanks, AC units, antennas, vents, railings, helipads, small gardens, rooftop signs.
4. **Street level:** awnings, shop signs, kiosks, lamps, railings, crosswalks, parked vehicles.
5. **Signage:** invented brands and words only; preset sign set for visitor marks.

Building families (`web/src/families.js`): every house tile gets one of six by fixed rules, so a building keeps its family while it stands: industrial halls (strip windows, sawtooth roofs, external pipes and frames, stacks) up to 10 floors beside the railway or the old works; old buildings (brick or weathered concrete, small deep windows, cornice, water tank, fire escape) in the old town and among the first generation; luxury towers (glass lobby, setback tiers with planted terraces, tall glass) at 20+ floors near a park or the centre; megastructures (layered off-centre sections with lit seams, an exoskeleton, skybridges to neighbours) for some 16+ floor towers near the centre; offices (lobby, framed curtain wall, a mechanical floor every dozen storeys, setbacks, a louvred plant room) at 10+ floors in glass sectors; apartment towers (slab, L or twin towers on a shop podium, balconies, bay windows, set-back penthouse) everywhere else. The seed varies a building within its family. Balconies, rooftop plant, pipes and planters are drawn only within about 400 m of the camera.

Rooftops (`web/src/roofs.js`): flat and modern, in the facade's own materials, never bright accent colours: parapets, mechanical penthouses set to one side, stepped crowns in the building's own facade, fields of cooling units, communication masts on equipment shelters, planted terraces with a glazed pavilion, utility roofs (tanks on frames, a cooling tower, ducts) and, rarely on tall towers, a distinctive crown (a ring of fins, a halo roof on posts, or a top cut at an angle). Each family has four roof types; neighbours of one family always differ (the type steps through the list by tile position), and the choice stays fixed while the building stands. The only sloped roof is a flat-topped slate mansard on about one old building in nine; no pointed roofs.

**Energy reactors and communication spires** (added 2026-11-06, reworked the same day: they were small cylindrical towers on raised square platforms; `web/src/rooftech.js`, modelled in Blender by `tools/blender/rooftop_tech.py`, ADR-0005): the power-generation systems of a vast dystopian civilization, massive, armoured and built into the tops of the towers, not props set on them. On towers of 8+ floors, by a hash of the tile: about 70% keep an ordinary dark crown, 20% an integrated mid-size reactor, 8% a larger industrial one and 2% a landmark one (22+ floors); a quarter of the industrial halls carry the industrial kind; old buildings never; a roof too small for a kind gets a smaller one or none. The three signature towers always carry a landmark reactor with a spire as its central mast (was: a lantern storey with a needle and violet halo rings). No platform: each housing rises straight out of the roof from a foundation sunk into it, braced out by buttress feet. Mid-size: a broad, low octagonal or hexagonal housing spanning 56 to 64% of the roof's shorter side (landmarks 62%, signature crowns 66%), about two-thirds as high as it is wide, a little taller on taller towers: heavy gunmetal ribs on the corners, chevron armour of blackened titanium on alternate faces, and between them the chamber shows as one tall recessed panel of light behind two heavy bars; a low cap closes over it, open in the middle onto the glowing chamber, which is the reactor's focal point from above. Industrial: a housing standing in a long armoured base with sloped walls, coolant drums and heat-exchanger fins. Landmark: a larger housing whose ribs rise past the cap and close in like claws, with a hotter chamber. The building's top is reworked round each one: an armoured containment crown on the roof edge in place of the parapet, buttresses from the housing's feet to the crown's corners, corner ribs carried down the top two floors, and an energy conduit glowing in a recessed channel down the middle of two faces. Mostly dark metal (midnight-blue steel `#1e2333`, graphite `#26282b`, weathered gunmetal `#464a51`, blackened titanium `#161c29`); the chamber is one large continuous emissive surface: deep crimson `#b3122a` with blood-red `#5a0e16` in its darker reaches, rich scarlet `#ff3a3a` where a slow, irregular noise field rises through it, small soft coral `#ff7a66` highlights at the hottest points (`palette.js` core*; corrected for AgX like the windows), never stripes or rows of lit "floors". It breathes once every fourteen seconds or so, each reactor out of step with its neighbours, and landmark cores now and then surge slowly; no flashing, and still with reduced motion. One shared material per kind, so every chamber in the city draws in one call. Communication spires (two designs, 7 to 14 m, about 24 m on the signature towers): a heavy base on a reinforced plinth, a tapered mast in square sections with collars, braces from the base, directional panels on arms, a transmitter array, a narrow tip and a red aviation beacon; light only from small crimson navigation lamps, a thin red seam set into one edge, now and then a cool-white technical light. They stand beside the shelter on antenna roofs, on the crowns of the landmark reactors (one in three, and every signature tower) and beside one industrial reactor in three where the roof leaves room, never through a housing. No floating rings or hoops anywhere. Glowing roof plates are gone too (changed 2026-11-06; was a white or amber lit plate on fin and halo crowns and under stepped crowns, which read as bright platforms from above): those crowns now carry a thin blood-red seam round the edge.

Each sector has a dominant family (glass, cream, navy, or pale glass), so districts read as neighborhoods; about one building in four is a teal or salmon accent. Buildings of 20+ floors are drawn as a full-width podium with a slimmer tower on top.

Built so far (`web/src/tiles.js`, `plant.js`, `city.js`): towers get a shop podium (lit shopfront, awning, sign, LED strip), a glass or concrete body with lit rooms, and on 20+ floors a setback crown with pink or cyan edges, antennas and sometimes a drone pad; construction tiles get a concrete core in yellow scaffolding with work lights and sometimes a tower crane; markets are cybernetics shops (below); plants are SECTOR 07 (below). Each tile's style seed picks the variant and turns the building to one of four facings. Streets have sodium lamps every block edge with soft light pools on the wet asphalt. Towers come in four shapes (plain, stepped, round, with a wing), carry vertical banners, rooftop billboards or holograms, and tall neighbours are sometimes joined by skybridges. Empty lots are small plazas with parked cars (some taxis) and kiosks. A blacked-out tile has every room dark and no neon or signs.

**Power plants** (`web/src/plant.js`, modelled in Blender by `tools/blender/power_plant.py`, ADR-0005): every plant tile is SECTOR 07, an invented design for a compact, aging power-generation block that reads as dangerous machinery, not entertainment. Its cyberpunk identity comes from architecture, density of machinery and wear, not neon: at least 85-90% of its surface is unlit worn metal and concrete (measured in Blender: about 0.1% glows). On the 12 m lot it gets its size from height: a reactor about 21 m tall (blackened concrete drum with buttresses and support frames, an armoured band with inspection slots behind grilles, a segmented gunmetal shell with bands of coil blocks and bolted armour, clamped coolant pipes, hatches, gauges, radiation warnings) is the focal point; two discharge towers of 35 and 29 m (welded collars, one painted hazard band each, vents, sooted heads, struts, maintenance platforms, steady red aviation lamps) come second. Round them: heavy headers and a pipe bridge, a windowless two-storey control block (roll shutter, a restricted access door in a hazard frame, control panel, conduits, junction boxes, a meter, a duct, a camera, roof fans, a small worn "SECTOR 07" plate), transformers with high-voltage plates. Light is only small dim amber maintenance lamps, cold-white indicators and red; no pink, no saturated cyan, no glowing outlines. Hazard markings only at the door, the apron, the towers' bands and rails. Steam from its vents is ambient life (browser-only, ADR-0002), not part of the model.

**Market shops** (`web/src/tiles.js` `market`, modelled in Blender by `tools/blender/market_hall.py`, ADR-0005): every market tile is a compact, heavily modified cybernetics and parts shop, two storeys under the old barrel roof, an invented design that reads as futuristic by its architecture even in flat daylight with every light off. Angular off-white and olive armour plates (corners cut at 45 degrees) overlap graphite walls and exposed steel sections; the outline is broken by a ventilation tower rising past the roof, a cooling rig with hoses, a fan unit, a maintenance platform, a tall corner sign turned off the facade, an asymmetric sign frame and a mast with dishes. Street level: a deep storefront in a thick angular frame under a layered armoured canopy on struts, a display of parts with a product hologram, a sliding security door, payment terminal, scanner and control panel, a vending terminal and a delivery locker. Each tile gets a shop from `SHOPS` in `world.js` (invented names, KUROTECH first): its name on the roof sign, its short mark on the corner sign, both white; "CYBERNETICS / PARTS / REPAIRS" in aged amber under the name. Light kept to three focal areas (roof sign, corner sign, storefront): white, aged amber, red indicators, a few cold cyan displays; no pink or purple, no outline strips. A market is its own shop, so it gets no street shopfront strip. Dark in a blackout, with no hologram.

**Corner storefronts** (`web/src/tiles.js` `storefront`, modelled in Blender by `tools/blender/storefront.py`, ADR-0005): the buildings on three corners where the central boulevard meets the next full street toward the opening view get, in place of the plain shop strip and on its footprint (a 14 m front, 3 m deep, one storey), a shop that belongs to a dense megacity rather than a modern street with a neon strip. A wall of graphite steel, not glass: a recessed mechanical entrance under a heavy lintel with a hazard band, braced titanium jambs, a segmented shutter half up under its drum and hydraulic arms; a display window behind steel bars and an angled armour plate; two vending terminals and a parcel locker set into the wall; a long steel canopy on diagonal struts with a shorter olive one stepped above it; conduits along the top; AC units and a fan on the roof; a dressed corner where the two fronts meet. Its light is small and dim: the shop inside, small work lamps under the canopy, amber and cyan screens, red indicators, and the shop's name (from `SHOPS`) on a screen over each door with its mark on one blade sign. Dark in a blackout. Other buildings get one of the street strips below.

**Billboards** (`web/src/city.js` `billboards`, a reusable kit modelled in Blender by `tools/blender/billboards.py`): heavy electronic signs bolted to tower walls on industrial frames, only on the corner towers of the busiest crossing for now. Three modules: a tall vertical display (3 x 12 m) in a box-section frame on a standoff truss with wall brackets, a caged ladder, a service platform and a lamp hood; a wide two-panel display (6 x 3.4 m) on raking brackets over a catwalk with two spotlights; and a double-sided blade (1.4 x 7 m) sticking straight out on two arms with a red beacon. Each is mounted near the corner, only where the tower's measured wall is big enough, else on its other street face or another corner tower. Each display shows a real advertisement (`posterTexture` in `textures.js`): an invented brand (`BRANDS` in `world.js`) and its product drawn as a picture in a soft glow over faint scanlines, with the brand, a slogan and a price tag. Six products for variety: a cyber arm (HALDEN), an eye implant (OKTAV), a cola can (ZEPHA), noodles (MOGU), a delivery drone (ORVEX) and a power cell (KAZEMURA), each brand used once. Each ad has two colours: off-white `#e8dcd3` or muted amber `#e09a45`, with red `#e2453c` or cold cyan `#8ccad2` on a few. No pink, purple or rainbow gradients. Dark in a blackout.

**Street props** (`web/src/city.js` `streetProps`, a reusable kit modelled in Blender by `tools/blender/street_props.py`): low-poly, functional street equipment on the busiest crossing's four corner sidewalks, three a corner: an electronic traffic sign (an amber dot-matrix message such as SPEED 40 or TOLL ACTIVE, facing the traffic coming in) or a camera pole (two cameras and a dome looking across the crossing) by the kerb, set back from the crosswalk; and two of a vending machine, a delivery locker bank, an electrical cabinet and a utility pedestal between bollards. They stand in a strip along the kerb so the walkway behind stays clear, and slide along the sidewalk to keep clear of lamps. Light is small: screens, status lights, red indicators. Dark in a blackout.

**Street strips** (`web/src/city.js` `shopfronts`, modelled in Blender by `tools/blender/shop_strips.py`): each street side of an ordinary lot is one of four one-storey strips, 14 m long, so neighbouring lots join into a varied street wall: shutter stalls (three roll-up bays, one down, one half up, one open, under a flat steel overhang), a food counter (an open kitchen under a sloping olive awning, stools, a menu screen, an exhaust duct up to the roof), a framed glass front (an exposed steel frame with braces, a corrugated canopy, a box sign), or an arcade (the shop set back behind a colonnade, small lamps in the soffit, screens on the beam). About four sides in ten keep the plain lit strip with its awning light. The lit shop windows come from the same shop facade everywhere; each strip is about a hundred triangles from afar, its small parts drawn only near the camera.

**Corporate signs** (`web/src/city.js` `corpSigns`, modelled in Blender by `tools/blender/corp_signs.py`, ADR-0005): sixteen large signs for eight invented corporations (`CORPS`: TARKO, OBELIN, VESTRA, MAREK, SOLMEN, IKARO, HELIX-9, KAISEN, each with its own mark: hex, bars, ring, chevron, triangle, eye, wave, grid) on the tallest towers of 14+ floors, at least 6 tiles apart, never on the billboard crossing, the signature towers, a skybridge's towers or the old town (its towers stand a little askew), only on a measured street wall big enough for them, and in place of the tower's own banner or wall projection so the two never overlap. Three kinds, stepped by tower position so neighbours differ and they sit at different heights (a tower gets the next kind that fits its wall): a crown standing on the roof line on legs bracketed down the wall (8 x 4 m), a pylon high on a wall (3.6 x 15 m, the name stacked), a band across a wall lower down (9 x 2.6 m). Each is heavy, weathered equipment: box-section frames, standoff trusses and bolted wall brackets, a panel back box with seams, lamp hoods, cable risers with junction boxes, a service ladder or catwalk, red status lights. The face is a weathered dark panel with the corporation's simple mark in deep crimson and its name and division in cold white; a separate muted amber dot-matrix display carries a status line. No rainbow colours, no pink. The displays are animated in their own shader (`materials.js` `liveSign`, no extra draw calls): the crimson mark breathes about once every four seconds, a soft scan band sweeps down each face every five or six, fine scanlines drift, once every eleven seconds one thin slice slips sideways for a quarter second, and the amber status display redraws left to right every five seconds. Each sign runs out of step with the others. Nothing flashes; with reduced motion there is no frame loop and the signs hold still. Dark in a blackout. About 30 draw calls for all sixteen.

**Ordinary signs and billboards** (`web/src/tiles.js` `dress`): the banner down a tower's face and the rooftop billboard carry odd ads for invented products (`ADS`: MEMORY BACKUP, RENT-A-LUNG, DREAMSAFE, SYNTH SUN, CLONE-LEGAL, OXY+, MINDLEASE, QUIET PILL), each a mark, a product name and a slogan in cold white, aged amber or muted cyan with a crimson, amber or white mark, on a display of their own (so the texture maps 0..1; on the old box it smeared to its edge colour) in a dark graphite frame, animated like the corporate signs. No pink or violet. Long names turn on their side on a banner.

**Projected ads** (`web/src/tiles.js` `projector`, hardware modelled in Blender by `tools/blender/holo_projectors.py`, ADR-0005): the ad is light in the air, not a panel. A rooftop projector (a long emitter housing on a finned plinth with two angled side heads, power units, a conduit, a red status light) throws an 8 x 4.5 m image above the roof; a wall projector (an emitter head on a braced arm, a cable down the facade) floats a 3.6 x 6 m image 2.4 m out from the wall, always at least 6 m up, clear of the shops. They take the place of every third tower's banner, half the rooftop billboards and all the old flat rooftop holograms; the rest keep their framed displays. Each shows an odd ad (`ADS`) on black, so only the mark and words glow, cast in the ad's ink (cold white, aged amber or muted cyan) in an additive hologram shader (`materials.js` `hologram`): rising scanlines, a refresh line climbing every few seconds, fine grain, a faint shimmer, soft edges, a slow breath, and every nine seconds one slice slipping sideways for a moment; fans of light rise from the lens, brightest at it, with slow drifting streaks. Far away they fade out rather than glow through the haze. Nothing flashes; with reduced motion they hold still. Dark in a blackout. About 16 draw calls.

**Skybridges over streets** (`web/src/city.js` `skybridge`, modules modelled in Blender by `tools/blender/skybridge.py`): two enclosed industrial corridors cross main streets between the tallest pairs of towers facing each other over one, outside the old town and away from the signature towers. A pair is used only where both towers have a wall at the bridge's height, measured from their parts, within 4 m of their lot's street edge and overlapping by the corridor's width, so a corridor never stops short of a set-back tower. Each is an end portal at each tower's wall (a deep collar with a hazard edge, a doorway, a service hatch, raking struts down to the facade) and 4 m bays between: heavy steel frame rings, graphite wall panels with olive cladding, a corrugated roof, an exposed truss under the floor, two utility pipes and a cable tray, a small lit window in every third bay. The floor sits on a floor line of both towers, about 40% of the lower one's height, well above the traffic. The smaller glass skybridges inside blocks are unchanged.

**Street level** (`web/src/streets.js`): main streets between sectors are 24 m: two 4 m raised sidewalks with curbs and a 16 m roadway with two lanes each way, dashed lane lines, a double centre line (a planted median on every other inner street, the avenues) and zebra crossings at every intersection; road paint glows faintly so it reads at night. Each sector is split both ways by 5 m strips, each either a service alley (asphalt, dumpsters) or a paved pedestrian lane with planters. Sodium lamps stand on both curbs about every 55 m with small, faint light pools. Building footprints vary with the style seed (small low-rises, towers filling most of the lot). Perimeter blocks: lots on a street or alley push their building up to it and carry a one-storey strip of shops with lit awnings the full lot wide, so streets have a continuous wall and each quarter-block keeps a courtyard; inner lots sit anywhere on theirs.

## 5. Ambient life

Generated in the browser, never simulated on the server, always consistent with the snapshot (`web/src/life.js` for traffic, `atmosphere.js` for rain, mist and clouds):

- Built (added 2026-11-07): **people on the streets** (`web/src/people.js`, figures in `web/src/figures.js`, ADR-0006). Ordinary citizens of a crowded, somewhat oppressive city, stylized but human (about 1.75 m, varied heights and builds), built from tapered boxes, short tubes and a small head, never capsules. ## 6. Motion and accessibility (non-negotiable)

- No flashing above 3 times per second, anywhere. Blinking lights stay at or below 1 per second.
- `prefers-reduced-motion`: no auto-rotate, no ambient movement, no particles in motion; the city remains fully readable.
- UI text meets contrast standards; never place text directly over the busy 3D scene without a panel.
- Meaning never relies on color alone (blackout = dark windows + label; construction = scaffolding + label).
- Orbit, zoom, timeline, and inspector are keyboard-operable. Works on mobile.

## 7. Performance targets

- 60 fps on a typical laptop, 30 fps on a mid-range phone.
- Instanced meshes for repeated objects (buildings, people, cars, props); texture atlases for facades.
- Pixel ratio capped at 2; a "low detail" toggle disables shadows and bloom.

## 8. Typography and copy

- Wordmark and data readouts in a monospace face; UI text in a readable sans.
- Sentence case everywhere. Invented names only.
